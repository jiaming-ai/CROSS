CROSS Nav BEV Atlas

Predicted and ground-truth bird's-eye views of held-out evaluation windows. Each grid is 400 × 400 cells of 5 cm around the current camera (centre, arrow = viewing direction), gravity-aligned. Light = free floor, dark = anything 0.1–1.8 m above it. Unknown ground truth shows the checkerboard. The thresholded prediction (P > 0.5) and geometric maps are compared with a soft planner using occupancy risk over the robot footprint. Goals are 2–8 m away, with a 0.2 m planning radius. Uncertainty is learned P(occupancy decision is wrong): black = 0, yellow = 1. The selective BEV leaves cells unknown above an uncertainty score of 0.2. Unknown ground truth cannot establish route safety; success uses the historical 0.1 m collision check. Low uncertainty alone does not establish obstacle-free space.
